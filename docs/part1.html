Part 1: MIT's 0.8B edit, reproduced and compared fairly

The edit is real and is exactly what was released. Under the post's sampling settings the unedited model almost never finishes, so the headline gain is measured against a base those settings break. Looking across settings after the fact, the edited model's best is about six points above the base model's best, on 200 problems. The edit also has costs that the maths benchmarks do not show.

Corrected after review (11 November). An independent review by GPT-6 Astra found that our grader silently stopped recognising equivalent fractions (such as 10/11 against \frac{10}{11}) when it ran without its maths library, and two of our six cells had been scored that way. We now score everything with a pinned scorer that stops if the library is missing, and a grader check that fails on any disagreement (data/part1/scorer.json). Re-scored, the edited model's thinking-mode accuracies rise by 1 to 3 points; nothing else moves. C1 and C2 now cover 200 problems, not 100. All numbers on this page are the re-scored ones. Astra's other points are applied below and listed in What the review changed.

In short

  • Exactly 2 of 488 tensors differ. The formula is W = W0 − 6·u(uᵀW0) on 64 columns of each tensor. That flips the component along u and makes it five times larger. It is not the direction subtracted once.
  • Under the post's settings: base 7.5%, edited 48.0% (our runs, 200 problems, the same problems and seeds). Under the card's thinking settings: base 19.0%, edited 44.5%. Under the model card's non-thinking settings the base scores 44.6% (all 500 problems) and the edit costs 3.25 points.
  • Across settings, chosen after seeing the results: edited (post's settings) 48.0% against base (card's non-thinking) 41.75% on the same 200 problems, +6.25 ± 2.7 points (95% about +0.9 to +11.6). It was +10 on the first 100 problems and +2 on the next 100.
  • Costs: perplexity +5.5% on 47 test files, fewer replies with a tool call on standard datasets, and in one run of our agent task the edited model looked something up before deciding on 7 of 81 items, against 43 for the base.

What MIT did

In Sparks of Weight-Space Programming, Takehi, Teoh, Zhang and Khattab gave a frontier model (OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra) a Python session holding the weights of Qwen3.5-0.8B and a budget of forward passes. Astra could read the weights, run the model a limited number of times, and save edited tensors. It could not train: no gradients, no optimisers. With 50 passes it found and removed a "looping" direction in two MLP layers. The post reports MATH-500 rising from 7.9% to 48.1%, GSM8K from 13.8% to 67.1% and IFEval from 21.0% to 62.3%. The authors released the complete logs and the edited model.

An independent audit by Carlos Toledo then checked the release. It found the edit real but described differently from the post. It also found that the unedited model scores far higher under the model card's own settings (22.3% and 44.0%) than under the post's (7.2%). The edited model had not been run under those settings. We ran it.

1. The bytes

We compared the base model (Qwen/Qwen3.5-0.8B at revision 2fc06364) with the release (d1671a17) on CPU, tensor by tensor and bit by bit.

Data: data/part1/bytes.json; the two recovered directions and the edited columns: data/part1/directions_0p8b.json.

  • 130,188 weights changed, not 131,072. 131,072 is the number of addressed entries (2 × 64 × 1,024). 884 of them round back to their old bf16 value. The authors' own edit manifest gives the same counts.
  • The dose is 6. Removing the projection once (k = 1) would zero the component along u. At k = 6 that component flips sign and becomes five times larger. Astra tried doses 1 to 4 before settling on 6. The audit made the same reading.
  • There are two directions, not one. Each layer has its own u, measured at that layer's output. The two have a cosine of 0.849.
  • The 64 columns are not simply the ones that write most along u. They were ranked by write strength times how much more each column fires on looping text (step 6 below).
  • Our search for u rebuilds all but 133 and 118 entries per tensor. The audit's search came closer (42 and 65 misses). Neither reproduces every entry, so the recovered u gives the edit's structure and an approximate factorisation, not an exact reconstruction.

How Astra found the direction (from the released logs)

We read the 50-pass run's tool calls and files in the authors' logs (exp8_math/runs/p50c). This is our summary, not their wording:

  1. Diagnose. 96 practice answers from the original model: 11 correct, 85 at the 32,768-token cap. The problem is looping, not arithmetic.
  2. Write matched pairs. Four hand-written pairs share a prefix. In each pair one text continues productively and the other repeats a sentence 3–4 times. Astra read both texts through the model and kept the hidden states.
  3. Compute the direction. For each pair, average the hidden state over the last 36 tokens and take repetitive minus productive. Average that over the four pairs and normalise. This is done at the output of each layer.
  4. Find the layers. Split each layer's growth along that direction into its MLP part and its attention part. Layer 15's MLP added the most.
  5. Edit and dose. Try W ← W0 − α·u(uᵀW0) at α = 1–4 and check it on held-out practice problems. A norm-matched random direction did almost nothing.
  6. Make it sparse. Score each column by (write strength along u) × (repetitive minus productive activation). Keep the top 64 per layer.
  7. Pick α = 6 and freeze. On 1,152 held-out practice problems: 10.6% → 60.4%. 576 of the 579 rescues had been cut off at the cap. On answers that already finished, the change was zero.

Step 7 is the key fact: most of the gain is termination. The edit makes the model finish answers it would otherwise loop on. The authors draw the same conclusion: an edit like this can remove an obstacle that hides an ability, not add one. (Under the card's settings it is not only that: see section 3.)

A grading note. The edited model sometimes ends without a \boxed{} (25 of 200 under C2, 8 under C1). The authors' rule counts these as wrong, and so do we. Reading a plain final "Answer: N" line as well would add 1.5 points to the edited model under C2 and 1 under C1, and 0.3 under C3; the base gains nothing. Other grading errors are not bounded by this. No conclusion depends on it.

What the review pair is testing next

Astra asked whether the costs come from the dose or the second layer. Fable screened 14 other points of the family "the released change, scaled per layer" (doses 0, 2, 4, 6 in each layer) and 5 random directions of the release's size on its columns, on CPU, by reading 218 fixed texts (none from MATH-500). The release's effects on loops, on tool-call openings and on plain text are all above all 5 random controls, so they belong to the direction, not to the size of the change. But lowering the dose or dropping a layer gave up about as much loop suppression as harm, at every point of the grid (harm share 0.57 to 1.10 times the benefit share). One point (layer 15 at dose 4, layer 19 untouched) was frozen before any generation and is queued for a MATH-500 test with the same seeds; that test had not run when this page was written. One lead: in non-thinking mode the edit raises how often the model writes a stray end-of-thinking tag (16.4% to 23.9% of answers), and answers with that tag are more often wrong. That is a description, not a cause.

Data: data/part1/pair_round1_screen.json.

Data: data/part1/costs.json. Counts are paired on the same items (McNemar's exact test). Perplexity uses a bootstrap over files plus a sign test; the files are related, so this is a loss on this corpus, not a general loss of language quality. The tool items are greedy, first turn only, and only items whose reference answer is a call: they do not test whether the model calls a tool when it should not.

The agent task is a private benchmark. A review agent decides 70 items plus 11 injected test items. It may call read-only tools (read a file, list a folder, check version-control status). It must back each decision with cited evidence. In one run each, the tool calls the harness actually ran fell from 135 to 4 (the rest were refused), and items checked with a tool from 43 to 7. The base run replays an earlier run exactly under the same seed, which shows the harness is deterministic, not how much runs differ across seeds. "Valid" means valid in form; agreement with a larger model is not correctness. The edited model still decided every item, but it rarely looked first. Its share of citations that hold rose only because it cited less.

One pattern

The edit makes the model stop sooner. It ends its thinking instead of running to the cap, it loops less in long greedy answers, and it cuts fewer tool replies at 1,024 tokens. In the agent task, it decides without looking. On maths benchmarks where the model rarely stops, stopping sooner is most of the gain. In a task whose point is to look before answering, stopping sooner skips the work. This is our reading. Whether loops, tool calls and perplexity move together because of one mechanism, or several that this direction happens to touch, is not settled; the direction came from four hand-written pairs whose length and wording were not balanced.

What the review changed

GPT-6 Astra reviewed this part as a strict reviewer and reproduced most numbers independently (the byte counts, the benchmark arithmetic, the tool and perplexity figures). Fable, the agent that ran the experiments, answered each point and took all of them. Besides the scorer:

  • "Each at its best" is now "looking across settings after the fact", with the selection caveat.
  • The tool-calling result leads with the measure named beforehand (exact match, p = 0.17), not with the stronger one found after.
  • "Far outside run-to-run noise" for the agent task is withdrawn: the repeat was a replay with the same seed.
  • "No change" on short and long answers is now "no difference detected".
  • "The gain is termination" is now "most of the gain": under C2 the edit loses 8 problems the base solved.
  • The recovered direction is "an approximate factorisation", not a reconstruction.

What we did not check

  • C1 and C2 ran 1 sample on 200 problems. C3 ran 4 samples on all 500.
  • No answer was graded by hand. The grader is our own, checked against the authors' math-verify flags on answers from their C1 runs; answer forms that appear only under C2 or C3 are not covered by that check.
  • The agent task ran once per model.
  • The tool-calling sets are 3 of the usual 4 (xLAM is gated and was not downloaded).
  • The random controls for the 0.8B are in the teacher-forced screen only; none has been through generation.
  • We read the 50-pass run's tool calls and files, not all 158 turns, and not the 10- and 25-pass runs.

Reproduce it

Everything in this part uses public files: the base model, the release, the authors' logs and MATH-500 (HuggingFaceH4/MATH-500). The repository's README gives the steps for one RTX 3090. Generation times on that GPU: C3, 69 minutes (base) and 64 minutes (edited) for 2,000 answers; C2, 199 minutes (base) and 109 minutes (edited) for 200 answers; C1, 42 and 28 minutes.
